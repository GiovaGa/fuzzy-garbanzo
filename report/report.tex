\section{Introduction}
The hierarchical facility location problem (HFLP) considered here is the two-level, globally-inclusive, nested formulation: demand nodes are assigned independently to an open Level-1 and to an open Level-2 facility, and Level-2 sites must be a subset of Level-1 sites. % 

We fix the maximum number of Level 1 and Level 2 facilities.
With this constraint, we minimize the following cost function:
\[ \sum_{i\in V} h_i \min_{j\in L_1} d_{ij} + \sum_{i\in V}\sum_j h_i \min_{j\in L_2} d_{ij} \]
where $h$ is the vector of demands, and $d$ is the matrix of distances.

\section{Methods}
This problem can be written as an Integer Linear Program (ILP), so we can use standard tools for this problem to evaluate other faster approaches, at least on small instances.

\subsection{Simulated Annealing (SA)}
SA maintains two sets of open facilities;  At each step a swap move replaces an open facility with a closed one (at the same level) or swaps within the nested constraint for Level-2.
The acceptance follows the Metropolis criterion with a fixed geometric cooling schedule.

\subsection{Genetic Algorithm (GA)}
The GA uses a population of candidate solutions, tournament selection, crossover and mutation operators adapted to the combinatorial structure. Convergence history is recorded for analysis.

\subsection{Instances and Experimental Setup}
We ran experiments on synthetic instances and real-data.

As artificial problems, we tested two topologies: Erdos-Reyni random graphs and a rectangular grid.

Real-data Veneto network: 559 comuni with demand equal to 2021 population values from ISTAT.

\section{Results}
Selected highlights and performance summaries follow. Full plots and detailed histories appear in the notebooks; here we report representative numbers.

\subsection{Artificial instances}
On the artificial instances we observed that both AI search techniques reach the ILP optimal value on small instances ($n=20$).

On larger instances ($n$ up to $400$), the ILP runtime becomes too large to be tractable. We tested a grid graph and a larger random graph. In both these cases the methods converge to within $X\%$ of each other.


\subsection{Veneto real-data instance}
We chose 21 Level 1 facilities (equal to the number of ULSS) and 5 Level 2 facilities.

This is the largest real instance tested. Results:
\begin{itemize}
    \item ILP produced an \textbf{optimal} solution with objective $~5.13\cdot 10^{10}$ and solve time ~71.9 s.
  \item Simulated Annealing found a solution matching the ILP objective in the run reported.
  \item Genetic Algorithm returned a solution with objective  $~5.24\cdot 10^{10}$ i.e. a \textbf{~2.06\%} gap from the best solution.
\end{itemize}
Representative Level-2 sites from the GA solution: Isola Vicentina, Lozzo di Cadore, Padova, Pescantina, Venezia.

\section{Conclusions}
Implementations of ILP, Simulated Annealing, and Genetic Algorithm for the globally-inclusive nested HFLP were developed and validated. ILP provides exact solutions for small instances and a useful reference when solvable; SA and GA are viable scalable alternatives for larger instances and real-world data. On the tested Veneto instance SA matched the ILP optimum while GA was within a few percent. Future work could include solver tuning, hybrid heuristics, capacity extensions, and benchmarking with stronger MIP solvers.
